L.A. Restaurant Owner Dies in Record Heat as
Insurance Fraud Plan Fails

Haldeman, 48, owner of "Haldeman's" in Los Angeles, was found dead at the Ranchero Motel. He got trapped in fresh tar while waiting for a phone call to prove he was not at his restaurant during a fire.

By Mary K. Calloway, Crime Correspondent  |  Published August 14, 1990, 6:00 AM  |  Reporting contributed by the Palm Springs Police Dept.

Illustration: "The ground did not feel solid under his feet." Haldeman's fatal walk across the Ranchero Motel courtyard. — PST Staff

Palm Springs: The owner of a local Restaurant died Sunday. He went there to make up an Alibi, while his restaurant burned down. The police say it was a very bad plan that went wrong. “Haldeman thought his plan to get insurance money was perfect,but everything went wrong…..”

Police said that at Friday night Haldeman drove to the Ranchero Motel. When his chef, José, accidentally started a fire,he wanted to be far away from L.A.. Haldeman broke the cooker himself. He wanted thath the fire would burn down the restaurant,and thath he would get money from the insurance company. He told everybody that his buisness was doing great and earning a lot of money, but this was false.

"He was very careful with the guest book. He wrote the date very clearly. I thought it was strange." — RICKY, MANAGER OF THE RANCHERO MOTEL

The plan began to go wrong when Haldeman noticed that the motel rooms didn’t had phones. This was a big problem,because he needed to get a phone call from his manayer,Larry Hyatt,to prove he was there. El único teléfono en todo el motel estaba al otro lado de un gran patio.

Haldeman no sabía que unos trabajadores habían trabajado en el suelo con alquitrán líquido fresco en el patio el día anterior. El alquitrán no se secó porque este día hacía mucho calor (más de 115 grados). Todo era líquido bajo una fina capa de arena. Haldeman corrió descalzo por el patio y pisó el alquitrán cuando el teléfono sonó a las 3:00 p.m..

“Fue una situación triste”, dijo la policía. Hyatt llamó 4 veces. En las primeras 3 llamadas, Haldemad estaba vivo pero estaba atrapado. En la cuarta llamada, no pudo soportar más el terrible calor. Mientras tanto, José estaba enfermo y no fue a trabajar ese día así que el restaurante no se quemó.

El plan de Haldeman se basaba en el cocinado de los libros llevado por Hyatt. Hyatt no sabía sobre el crimen. La policía dice que Hyatt "never sospechó nada", porque creía en la información falsa que Haldeman le decía.

Inside the Haldeman Case: 7 Questions Answered

Click to reveal answers
Why did Haldeman want to burn his restaurant?+
Haldeman had no money. His restaurant was losing customers, and he had many debts from his divorce and gambling. He wanted to burn the restaurant to get money from the insurance company.
Who was Larry Hyatt and was he a criminal?+
Larry Hyatt was the business manager. He was not a criminal. He kept the records, but he did not know they were fake. He believed Haldeman was telling the truth.
How did Haldeman plan to start the fire?+
He damaged the gas pipes in the kitchen. He hoped that when the chef, Jose, turned on the stove, the gas would explode and start a fire.
Why did Haldeman need a phone call from Hyatt?+
He needed the call to prove he was in Palm Springs. If Hyatt called him at the motel, it would show that Haldeman was not in Los Angeles during the fire.
Why did the plan fail in both cities?+
In L.A., the chef was sick and did not go to work, so there was no fire. In Palm Springs, Haldeman got stuck in fresh tar while trying to answer the phone and died from the heat.
Why is the hot weather important in the story?+
The heat made the fresh tar liquid. If it was not so hot, the tar would be hard, and Haldeman could walk on it. The heat was the reason he got trapped and died.
What is the main lesson of the story?+
The lesson is that greed and bad actions can lead to a terrible end. Haldeman thought he was smarter than everyone else, but his own plan caused his death.

Vocabulary Dossier: Words from the Scene

Eight key terms identified in Cooking the Books — defined and put to use.

Financial ruin
Noun phrase
A situation where a person loses all their money and cannot pay their debts.
"He was facing financial ruin" — Haldeman had no money to pay his rent or his debts.
Alibi
Noun
Proof that a person was in a different place when a crime happened.
Haldeman needed the phone call to confirm his alibi — proof he was in Palm Springs, not L.A.
Trusting
Adjective
Believing that other people are good and honest.
Hyatt was a trusting man — he never suspected that Haldeman was lying to him.
Tar
Noun
A thick, black, sticky substance used for making roads.
The fresh tar in the courtyard was liquid because of the extreme heat.
Succumbed
Verb
To die from a disease, injury, or extreme conditions.
Haldeman succumbed to the fierce heat while trapped in the tar.
Unravel
Verb
When a plan or situation starts to fail or fall apart.
The plan began to unravel when he realized there were no phones in the rooms.
Forged
Adjective
Fake or made to look real in order to deceive people.
Haldeman used forged records to show his business was making money.
Arson
Noun
The crime of intentionally starting a fire to damage a building.
The police investigated the fire as a case of arson for insurance money.
