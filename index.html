L.A. Restaurant Owner Dies in Record Heat During Desert Trip

Police describe a "flawed plan" involving insurance fraud and a motel alibi that ended in tragedy.

By Arthur Miller, Staff Reporter
THE SCENE: A digital recreation of the courtyard where Haldeman was found.

Palm Springs: The owner of a local Restaurant died Sunday. He went there to make up an Alibi, while his restaurant burned down. The police say it was a very bad plan that went wrong. “Haldeman thought his plan to get insurance money was perfect,but everything went wrong…..”

Police said that at Friday night Haldeman drove to the Ranchero Motel. When his chef, José, accidentally started a fire,he wanted to be far away from L.A.. Haldeman broke the cooker himself. He wanted thath the fire would burn down the restaurant,and thath he would get money from the insurance company. He told everybody that his buisness was doing great and earning a lot of money, but this was false.

"He was very careful with the guest book. He wrote the date very clearly. I thought it was strange." — RICKY, MANAGER OF THE RANCHERO MOTEL

The plan began to go wrong when Haldeman noticed that the motel rooms didn’t had phones. This was a big problem,because he needed to get a phone call from his manayer,Larry Hyatt,to prove he was there. The only phone in the whole motel was across a big courtyard.

Haldeman didn’t know that workers had worked on the ground with fresh liquid tar in the courtyard the day before. The tar did not dry because this day it was so hot (over 115 degrees). It was all liquid under a thin layer of sand. Haldeman runed barefoot trought the courtyard and stepped into the tar when the phone rang at 3:00 p.m..

“It was a sad situation,” police said. Hyatt called 4 times. On the first 3 calls, Haldemad was alive but was trapped. On the fourth call, he couldnt stand the terrible heat any longer. Meanwhile, José was sick and didnt go to work that day so the restaurant didnt burn down.

Haldeman’s plan was based on the cook of the books kept by Hyatt. Hyatt didn’t knew about the crime. Police say thet Hyatt "never suspected anything", because he belived in the false information that Haldeman told him.
